\section{Information outside the book}
\label{ch:external}

The last family does not change the learner; it changes what the learner is shown.  Once
the stateless control and the stateful model read the same sixty-one features and tie at one
hour (\cref{sec:stateful:calibration}), the remaining question is whether anything the book
does not contain predicts the price: a pretrained forecaster's view of the price series, the
news flow about the market's subject, or the text of the market itself.

\subsection{Time-series foundation models}
\label{sec:external:tsfm}
\label{sec:exp:tsfm}


\paragraph{Zero-shot.}  Chronos-2 was given, for 60{,}000 random validation samples per
horizon, the last 512 mids of the market on a one-minute grid (1h) or five-minute grid
(12h), ending at the sample time, and asked for the quantiles of the mid at $t+H$.
\Cref{tab:exp:tsfm} turns the forecast into a decision in several ways.  The sign of
the median almost never predicts \textsc{const} (17\% of the samples at 1h against 55\%
in the labels) and scores $0.470$; with a half-tick dead zone it reaches $0.505$, still
$-2.6$ [$-3.4$, $-1.9$] below Mean Reversion at 1h and $-0.4$ [$-1.4$, $+0.5$] at 12h.
Where Mean Reversion takes a side, Chronos agrees with it 65--68\% of the time; where
they disagree and the price moves, Chronos is right 52.9\% of the time at 1h and 48.0\%
at 12h.  The foundation model re-discovers mean reversion in context.

\paragraph{As features.}  The same quantiles were then cached for every active market
on a five-minute grid over 6--13 March (2.6--3.1 million forecasts per day, context of 512
one-minute mids ending strictly before the grid time) and given to the best 1h model as
eleven extra inputs: the ten-fold distance of the 10/50/90\% quantiles at 15, 30 and 60
minutes from the \emph{current} mid, the sign of the 60-minute median change and an
availability flag; a forecast is used for at most eight minutes.  They enter both the
order-book window and the direct path (\cref{sec:models:tsfm}).
\Cref{tab:exp:tsfmfeat} compares the two runs on the same validation samples.  The gain
on all samples is $+0.29$ points, ten times the seed spread of the base model and with an
interval that excludes zero, but it vanishes on the deduplicated samples and is the net
of two opposite effects: $+0.85$ in the central price range and $-0.76$ at the extremes,
where the zero-shot study had already shown the forecasts to be noise.  The direct path
reads one forecast feature for direction, the sign of the 60-minute median (the largest
directional weight in the layer, read \emph{with} the forecast), and uses the width of
the quantile band for the \textsc{const} logit; the lookback weights are unchanged to two
decimals.  A second seed of both runs gives the same picture ($+0.57$ [$+0.33$, $+0.82$]
on all samples, $+0.93$ central, $-0.16$ extreme, $+0.14$ deduplicated): the gain on the
full stream and in the central range is reproducible, the loss at the extremes keeps its
sign but not its size.

\subsubsection{Why the foundation model re-discovers mean reversion}
\label{sec:analysis:tsfm}


Chronos-2 standardises each context window and forecasts its continuation.  On a
one-tick process the standardised window is noise around a level, and the model's
best in-context guess is a return to the level: its median moves against the last change
68\% of the time Mean Reversion does.  It is worse than the rule for two reasons: it
never predicts ``no change'' (a dead zone recovers most of the gap), and at extreme
prices the per-window standardisation amplifies one-tick noise into forecasts that are
8 points below the rule.  This is the finance literature's finding
\cite{rahimikia2025revisiting,dewage2026hybrid} reproduced on prediction markets: value,
if any, must come from a learned correction on top of the frozen forecast.  That is the
experiment of \cref{sec:models:tsfm}, and its outcome
(\cref{tab:exp:tsfmfeat}) fits the same picture.  Given to the best model as inputs, the
forecasts add about $0.9$ points where prices are central (both seeds) and cost between
$0.2$ and $0.8$ where they are extreme, for a net $+0.3$ to $+0.6$ on the full stream and
nothing reliable on independent samples.  The
direct path learns to use a single bit of the forecast, the sign of the 60-minute median
change, with the largest directional weight in the layer; the rest of the information
it extracts is the width of the quantile band, which it reads as the probability of
``no change''.  In other words, the foundation model is used as a smoothed second opinion
on the direction and as a volatility estimate, both of which the recurrent state already
has in the central range and both of which are unreliable at the boundaries.  The
natural fix, masking the forecast outside the central range or giving the direct path
the price level so that it can do the masking itself, was not run.

\subsection{News volume}
\label{sec:external:news}

The twelve news features of \cref{sec:prep:features}, added to the control's table, change
nothing: $-0.10$ [$-0.20$, +0.00] on all
samples, $-0.06$ [$-0.16$, +0.04] on the political markets, which are 38\% of the samples, and
$-0.27$ on the rows whose market has no entity at all, the signature of noise features displacing
useful splits; the twelve features take 3\% of the trees' gain.  At one hour the news flow about a
market's entities is either already in the book or unrelated to the contract's question, and a
finer alignment of articles to questions is a different project.  The deep model was therefore
not run with these inputs.

\subsection{The text of the market}
\label{sec:external:text}

The sentence embedding of the question, as used to initialise the market state, has no
effect (\cref{sec:data:tensors}); what the text could contribute is the \emph{type of
contract} (\cref{sec:expl:templates}).  Questions are templated (3{,}528 templates among the active markets), and
the template explains 55\% of the between-market variance of the probability of a move
within one hour, from 4\% for House-seat markets to 83\% for daily-temperature markets;
a ridge regression on the embedding predicts that probability out of sample with
$R^2 = 0.22$, no better than the template means.  The cheap way to use the text is a
learned embedding of the category or template fed to the predictor; the control gets a
fifth of a point from the static fields, on top of everything else.

\subsection{What is outside the book}

The three sources give one answer at one hour.  A frozen forecaster of the price series
knows what the market's own history knows, mean reversion and a volatility estimate, and is
useful only as a smoothed second opinion in the central price range; the news flow about a
market's entities is either already in the book or unrelated to the contract's question; and
the text matters through a handful of contract types.  Information outside the book that
would move a one-hour prediction, the reference price of a crypto interval market
\cite{young2026openmarket}, the score of a game, the content rather than the volume of the
news, was not available to this study, and \cref{sec:horizons:where} shows where its absence
is felt.
