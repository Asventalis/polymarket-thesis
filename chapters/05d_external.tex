\section{Information outside the book}
\label{ch:external}

The last family does not change the learner; it changes what the learner is shown.  Once
the stateless control and the stateful model read the same sixty-one features and tie at
one hour (\cref{sec:stateful:residual}), the remaining question is whether anything the
book does not contain predicts the price: a pretrained forecaster's view of the price
series, the news flow about the market's subject, or the text of the market itself.

\subsection{A pretrained forecaster, zero-shot and as features}
\label{sec:external:tsfm}
\label{sec:exp:tsfm}

\Cref{tab:exp:tsfm} gives Chronos-2 zero-shot on 60{,}000 random validation samples per
horizon, with the forecast turned into a decision in the ways of \cref{sec:models:zeroshot}.
The sign of the median almost never predicts \textsc{const} (17\% of the samples at one hour
against 55\% in the labels) and is far below the rule; with a half-tick dead zone it recovers
most of the gap and stays below Mean Reversion at one hour, within the interval of it at
twelve.  Where Mean Reversion takes a side, the forecaster agrees with it two times in
three; where they disagree and the price moves, it is right about half of the time.  The
foundation model re-discovers mean reversion in context, and nothing more.

As inputs of the best one-hour model (\cref{tab:exp:tsfmfeat}), the eleven forecast
features of \cref{sec:prep:features} add a gain on all samples that is ten times the seed
spread of the base model and has an interval that excludes zero, but it vanishes on the

range and a loss at the extremes, where the zero-shot study had already shown the forecasts
to be noise.  A second seed of both runs gives the same picture; the gain on the full stream
and in the central range is reproducible, the loss at the extremes keeps its sign but not
its size.  The direct path reads one bit of the forecast for direction, the sign of the
60-minute median, and the width of the quantile band for the \textsc{const} logit, while its
lookback weights are unchanged to two decimals.

\paragraph{Why the forecaster re-discovers mean reversion.}
\label{sec:analysis:tsfm}
Chronos-2 standardises each context window and forecasts its continuation.  On a one-tick
process the standardised window is noise around a level, and the model's best in-context
guess is a return to the level, which is what the rule predicts.  It is worse than the rule
for two reasons: it never predicts ``no change'', and at extreme prices the per-window
standardisation amplifies one-tick noise into forecasts that are several points below the
rule.  This is the finding of the finance literature
\cite{rahimikia2025revisiting,dewage2026hybrid} reproduced on prediction markets: value,
if any, must come from a learned correction on top of the frozen forecast, and here the
learned correction uses the forecast as a smoothed second opinion on the direction and as a
volatility estimate, both of which the recurrent state already has in the central range and
both of which are unreliable at the boundaries.  Masking the forecast outside the central
range, or giving the direct path the price level so that it can do the masking itself, was
not run.

\subsection{News volume}
\label{sec:external:news}

The twelve news features of \cref{sec:prep:features}, added to the control's table, change
nothing: a tenth of a point lost on all samples, the same on the political markets, which are
38\% of the samples, and a quarter of a point lost on the rows whose market has no entity at
all, the signature of noise features displacing useful splits; the twelve features take 3\%
of the trees' gain.  At one hour the news flow about a market's entities is either already
in the book or unrelated to the contract's question, and a finer alignment of articles to
questions is a different project.  The deep model was therefore not run with these inputs.

\subsection{The text of the market}
\label{sec:external:text}

The sentence embedding of the question, used to initialise the market state, has no
measurable effect on the predictions (\cref{sec:data:tensors}); what the text could
contribute is the \emph{type of contract} (\cref{sec:expl:templates}).  Questions are
templated, 3{,}528 templates among the active markets, and the template explains 55\% of
the between-market variance of the probability of a move within one hour, from 4\% for
House-seat markets to 83\% for daily-temperature markets; a ridge regression on the
embedding predicts that probability out of sample with $R^2 = 0.22$, no better than the
template means.  The cheap way to use the text is therefore a category or template
indicator, and the control gets a fifth of a point from the static fields, on top of
everything else.

\subsection{What is outside the book}

The three sources give one answer at one hour.  A frozen forecaster of the price series
knows what the market's own history knows, mean reversion and a volatility estimate, and is
useful only as a smoothed second opinion in the central price range; the news flow about a
market's entities is either already in the book or unrelated to the contract's question; and
the text matters through a handful of contract types.  Information outside the book that
would move a one-hour prediction, the reference price of a crypto interval market
\cite{young2026openmarket}, the score of a game, the content rather than the volume of the
news, was not available to this study, and \cref{sec:horizons:where} shows where its absence
is felt.
