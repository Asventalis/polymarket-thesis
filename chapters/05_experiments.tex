\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:experiments}

This chapter reports the experiments.  \Cref{sec:data:protocol} gives the setup under
which every number is produced: the periods, the metric, the subsets, the intervals, the
calibration rule and the compute.  \Cref{sec:main:results} then gives the main results
first: the best model of every family at every horizon, on the validation day and on the
locked test days, and every model of the study at one hour, in two tables that compare the
families side by side.  The families are then assessed one at a time, in the order of
\cref{ch:models} and mostly at one hour, the horizon at which the design was done, each with
the few results that explain its place in the main tables: rules and stateless learners
(\cref{ch:stateless}), the stateful model (\cref{ch:stateful}), the graph models
(\cref{ch:graph}) and the sources outside the book (\cref{ch:external}).
\Cref{ch:horizons} reads the same families across the five horizons, by kind of market and
for robustness, and \cref{sec:horizons:test} reports the locked test set.
\Cref{sec:exp:econ} asks what a direction call is worth once it is traded, with a simple model
of the costs and a light simulation of the calls as a taker and as a maker.  The ablations and
the detailed results that the chapter does not need are in \cref{app:results}; the
discussion of the results against the four questions of the thesis is in
\cref{ch:conclusions}.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:data:protocol}
\label{sec:models:protocol}

Models are trained on 6--12 March and validated on 13 March, with 24--26 March locked for
the final numbers (\cref{sec:data:splits,sec:horizons:test}); hyper-parameters and design
choices were selected on the validation day.  All numbers in the thesis are balanced
accuracies \cite{brodersen2010balanced} on the validation day, unless stated otherwise.
Every result is reported on all validation samples and on three subsets: the
\emph{deduplicated} samples (at most one per market per ten minutes, 1.2--1.3 million), the
\emph{extreme} ones (mid outside $[0.05, 0.95]$) and the \emph{central} ones.  Differences
between models are computed on exactly the same samples and carry a 95\% hour-block
bootstrap interval in brackets (\cref{sec:bg:eval}); ``MR'' is Mean Reversion-$H$.  Three
rules make the comparisons fair.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{The same samples.}  The stateless learners are trained and evaluated on the
  samples of the stateful runs: at one hour the control was built from the run's predictions;
  at one and fifteen minutes it had to be rebuilt on the run's samples, because a control
  that samples tensor rows at the row time is a different population
  (\cref{sec:horizons:short}).
  \item \textbf{The same calibration.}  The decision rule of a classifier trained with a
  balanced loss is not balanced-accuracy-optimal on a different class mix.  Every model is
  therefore given two logit offsets (on \textsc{down} and \textsc{up}) chosen on the even
  validation hours and applied to the odd ones and vice versa, on a grid of $\pm 0.6$ in
  steps of $0.1$ at one hour and $\pm 2$ at the short horizons, and models are compared
  after this calibration.  The offsets are worth three tenths of a point to the trees at one
  hour and more than five at one minute; a comparison without them measures thresholds, not
  models.
  \item \textbf{Full-length runs.}  Where a short experiment was needed to screen many
  variants, it was trained on 11--12 March; every claim rests on full-length runs, and the
  cases in which a short test predicted the wrong sign are reported
  (\cref{sec:horizons:robustness}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Compute and the order of the experiments.}  Every stateful run trains on one
GPU of a shared server, one run per GPU, for nine to seventeen hours per pass depending on
the horizon and the load (\cref{app:runs}); the stateless learners train in minutes on a CPU.
Because of that cost the design was done at one hour, variants were screened with two-day
short tests and confirmed with full-length runs, the other horizons were run with the final
configurations, and seeds were repeated where a gain was small.  Every run is identified by
a label that encodes its configuration and writes its validation predictions to disk, so
that any two runs can be compared on exactly the same samples (\cref{app:runs}).

\section{Main results}
\label{sec:main:results}
\label{sec:main:synthesis}
\label{sec:horizons:synthesis}

\Cref{tab:horizons:synthesis} puts the best model of each family at each horizon on one
page, on the validation day; \cref{tab:test:main} in \cref{sec:horizons:test} repeats it on
the locked test days, and \cref{fig:horizons:synthesis} draws the gain of the stateful
family over the stateless control at each horizon.  Three regimes appear, and the rest of
the chapter explains them.  At one minute the task is activity, calibration is worth five
points to every model, and the sequence of book events carries information about the next
minute of a quiet market that the summary features lose: the stateful model beats the
calibrated trees alone and, by more, as their residual, with intervals that exclude zero on
every subset, and its gain over the tabular network on the same features is concentrated on
the sparse markets.  At fifteen minutes and one hour the summary is nearly complete: the
trees, the tabular network and the stateful model given the same inputs and the trees as a
base are within a point of one another, the online, time-ordered regime costs the stateful
model one to two points unless it is given the trees as a base, the residual of the trees
is still seven tenths above them at fifteen minutes and ties them at one hour, and the graph
and the external sources add nothing.  At six and
twelve hours the task is the direction of a slow reversal read together with the price
level and the market's lifecycle, the stateless control is six points above the rule, and
the stateful models of this study are below it, at twelve hours even when they read the
engineered features and sit on the trees as a base.  The same architecture cannot be the right one at both ends, and the reading the
data give is that a model of this market should be a stateless learner on the activity
clock, the first level, the long returns, the price level and the contract type, with a
sequential state added only where the horizon is shorter than the summary.
