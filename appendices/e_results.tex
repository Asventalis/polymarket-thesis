balanced accuracy on the validation day unless stated otherwise, differences in points with

\section{Loss and imbalance variants}
\label{app:results:loss}
\label{sec:stateful:variants}

With the LOB-only lookback model as reference, \cref{tab:exp:variants} compares the fixed
class weights with automatic (inverse-frequency) weights, with undersampling of the
\textsc{const} class during training, and with regression of the signed change followed by
a threshold calibrated on the last training day.  At one and six hours the fixed weights are
best; at twelve hours, where \textsc{const} is a third of the samples instead of a half,
undersampling gains five points.  Regression is far worse at every horizon: the Huber loss
is dominated by the few large moves and the threshold calibrated on training data does not
transfer.  The continuous-time anti-symmetric update \cite{gravina2024ctan} in place of the
GRU cell gives no gain and is much worse without the decay; the wallet behaviour features
give $\pm 0.3$ points with no consistent sign.

\section{Follow-up runs of the model with the engineered features}
\label{app:results:followup}

Two follow-up runs, each paired against the run with the engineered features of
\cref{sec:stateful:hand}, exclude the two obvious explanations of the gap that remains to
the trees (\cref{tab:app:followup}).  Training with the control's objective, a
class-balanced cross-entropy in place of the focal loss, and a predictor head four times
wider and one layer deeper both lose, and both lose exactly where the features had gained
most, the deduplicated and the extreme samples; both predict fewer moves than the focal-loss
run, a class mix within a point of the labels', and on the sparse subsets that is what
balanced accuracy punishes.  With two logit offsets fitted crosswise on the validation
hours the three runs become indistinguishable: the loss and the head moved the decision
bias of the model, not the function it learned.  The prior correction of
\textcite{menon2021logitadjustment}, consistent for the balanced error when the
probabilities are calibrated, is destructive on this focal-loss model ($0.527$): the focal
weights already shift the probabilities towards the rare classes and the correction counts
the shift twice.

\section{Weight averaging}
\label{app:results:ema}

An exponential moving average of the weights along the training pass (decay $0.998$ and
$0.999$ per optimiser step, horizons of about $500$ and $1{,}000$ steps out of $3{,}000$),
swapped in before validation, does not help on its own (\cref{tab:app:ema}): both runs are
below the run with the features on all samples, with a decision bias towards \textsc{down}
that two cross-fitted offsets only partly remove, and both tie the trees on the deduplicated
samples.  What the averaged weights change is their complementarity: combined with LightGBM
by a geometric mean of the probabilities (weight cross-fitted on the validation hours) they
give three times the gain of the run with the features on the deduplicated samples.  The
sequential state is worth most where the trees are weakest, on the sparse markets, and the
smoother weights carry that part better than the last ones.

\section{The cost of the gap between validation and test}
\label{app:results:lag}

The periods of the study are not contiguous (\cref{sec:data:splits}).  To measure what the
gap costs, the stateless control was refitted with the same features, hyper-parameters and
protocol on two other periods and evaluated on the same test samples as the control of
\cref{sec:horizons:test}: on every day from 6 to 20 March, with early stopping on 21 March
(no unused day before the warm-up), and on the week adjacent to the test alone, 14--20 March,
with the same early-stopping day.  \Cref{tab:app:lag} gives the three fits at every horizon,
as raw decisions, with the paired differences from the fit of the thesis and their 95\%
hour-block bootstrap intervals over the test hours.  Fitting on every day gains between half
a point and one point of balanced accuracy on the test days at every horizon, with intervals
that exclude zero everywhere.  Fitting on the adjacent week alone gains a few tenths on all
samples, not distinguishable from zero at one hour and at twelve hours, and between half a
point and almost a point on the deduplicated samples, close to what every day gives there.
On the full stream, dominated by the busy markets, what the extra days bring is therefore
mostly more data; on the sparse markets the week adjacent to the test recovers most of the
gain on its own, so there recency matters as much as quantity.  Either way the gap costs the
control about half a point to one point, small against the distances between the families.

\section{The economic simulation in detail}
\label{app:results:econ}

\Cref{tab:app:econ} extends \cref{tab:econ:pnl} to the stateful models whose test predictions
exist at each horizon, to the oracle that calls the realised label, and to the subsets: all
calls, the deduplicated ones (at most one per market per ten minutes), the most confident
